# Teaching Guide — Lesson 3: The Three Questions

*The Three Fundamental Principles (Thalathat al-Usul for Kids) · SunnahSaplings*
*Sample draft for owner review. Every scholar quote is a verified quote; English lines are meanings, not word-for-word translations.*

## Goal of the lesson
By the end, your child can say the three foundations every Muslim must know (knowing their Lord, their religion and their Prophet ﷺ) and colour the date-palm page.

## Background for the parent (2 minutes)
Shaykh al-Islam Muhammad ibn Abdul-Wahhab wrote *Thalathat al-Usul* to teach these foundations. Shaykh Ibn Baz and Shaykh al-Uthaymeen both explained it. In this lesson your child learns the three foundations themselves; later lessons explain each one.

## From the Scholars (commentary)

**1. The three foundations: the words of the original text, as quoted by Shaykh Ibn Baz**

> يتفرع

Meaning: "If you are asked: what are the three foundations every person must know? Say: knowing your Lord, your religion, and your Prophet Muhammad ﷺ."
Source: *Sharh Thalathat al-Usul*, Shaykh Abdul-Aziz Ibn Baz (Dar al-Masir, 1st ed., 1418 AH), p. 38

**2. Why these three? Shaykh Ibn Baz**

> يتفرغ

Meaning: "These three foundations gather the whole religion: Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is your Prophet? These are what a person is asked about in the grave."
Source: *Sharh Thalathat al-Usul*, Shaykh Abdul-Aziz Ibn Baz (Dar al-Masir, 1st ed., 1418 AH), p. 38

**3. What "usul" means: Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen**

> فَإِذَا قِيلَ لَكَ: مَا الأُصُولُ الثَّلاثَةُ التِي يَجِبُ عَلَى الإِنْسَانِ مَعْرِفَتُهَا؟ فَقُلْ: مَعْرِفَةُ الْعَبْدِ ربه ودينه ونبيه محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم.

Meaning: "'Usul' means foundations: what other things are built on, like the foundation of a wall, or the root of a tree that the branches grow from."
Source: *Sharh Thalathat al-Usul*, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen (Dar al-Thurayya, 4th ed., 1424 AH), p. 42
(Printed with «يتفرع»; the digital copy has a typing slip «يتفرغ».)

**4. The ayah Shaykh al-Uthaymeen cites: Surah Ibrahim 14:24**

> أَلَمۡ تَرَ كَيۡفَ ضَرَبَ ٱللَّهُ مَثَلٗا كَلِمَةٗ طَيِّبَةٗ كَشَجَرَةٖ طَيِّبَةٍ أَصۡلُهَا ثَابِتٞ وَفَرۡعُهَا فِي ٱلسَّمَآءِ ٢٤

Meaning: "See you not how Allâh sets forth a parable? A goodly word as a goodly tree, whose root is firmly fixed, and its branches (reach) to the sky (i.e. very high)." (*The Noble Qur'an*, Hilali-Khan). Arabic text: King Fahd Complex (Madinah) mushaf.

## Say it like this (script)
1. "Today we learn three foundations. A foundation is like the roots of a tree. Shaykh al-Uthaymeen said the roots are what the branches grow from."
2. "Every Muslim must know three things: who is my Lord, what is my religion, and who is my Prophet ﷺ."
3. "Shaykh Ibn Baz said these three gather the whole religion."
4. Point to the coloring page: "Let's colour the three roots: Rabbī, Dīnī, Nabiyyī ﷺ."

## If your child asks…
- **"Why are they called roots?"** Shaykh al-Uthaymeen explained that *usul* means foundations, like the root of a tree that the branches grow from (*Sharh Thalathat al-Usul*, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen (Dar al-Thurayya, 4th ed., 1424 AH), p. 42).
- **"Why only three?"** Shaykh Ibn Baz said these three gather the whole religion (*Sharh Thalathat al-Usul*, Shaykh Abdul-Aziz Ibn Baz (Dar al-Masir, 1st ed., 1418 AH), p. 38).
- **Answers to the three questions themselves** come in the next lessons, from the scholars' explanations. Until then: "We will learn each one in the next lessons, in sha Allah."

## Review game: Root Match
Write رَبِّي, دِينِي, نَبِيِّي ﷺ on three paper "roots". Say the English (my Lord / my religion / my Prophet ﷺ) and your child puts a paper leaf on the matching root.
