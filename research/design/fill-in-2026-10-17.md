## 1. Common patterns in the market

| Pattern | What the evidence shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Word bank under/next to the sentences** | The standard K–2 format. Twinkl's Five Pillars fill-in sheet uses a word bank; TpT first-grade "fill in the blank with word bank" sight-word sets rate ~4.8/5 with 46–250 ratings; a free 1st–3rd grade compound-word sheet with word bank: 4.86/5 (22 ratings). | F1, F2 |
| **Fill-in inside Islamic workbooks** | Goodword Hadith Activity Book: each chapter ends with "fill in the blanks" and "match the following". Etsy prophets bundles include true/false and fill-in-the-blank. "All About Me / My Faith" sheets use fill-in prompts. | F3, F4 |
| **Tick / circle the right answer** | An Etsy "Pillars of Faith – Iman" preschool sheet ($2.00) is a tick-the-right-answer sheet. | F5 |
| **Number of choices** | Assessment guidance: 4–5 choices for older grades, **as few as 3 for K–1**; keep the count the same within a set; distractor *plausibility* matters more than count for young children. | F6 |
| **Write-in space** | A workbook printing guide warns that pages that look fine on screen crowd handwriting once printed, and recommends **≥0.75 in per answer line for early handwriting**. OT rubric: 3/4 in lines for grade 1, 5/8 in for grade 2. | F7, F8 |
| **Answer keys** | Answer keys are rare in Etsy Islamic listings (only one explicit mention found, a Ramadan book for ages 3–5). Buyers of other curricula complain when keys don't match the questions. | F4, F9 |

## 2. What buyers praise and complain about

- **Praise (snippets):** Twinkl fill-in sheet called "lovely … supporting children's learning about
  the five pillars" (short reviewer comment) (F1). TpT word-bank sets hold ~4.8/5 (F2).
- **Complaints (general workbooks, not Islamic):** answer keys that "do not always match the
  answers listed"; answer keys locked behind paywalls; not "printer friendly" (F9).
- **Educator warnings:** crowded pages and too little writing room are the main design failures
  (F7, F10). Multiple-choice asks a young child to hold several options in memory; keep options
  few and short (F10).
- **Islamic-specific:** reviewers question accuracy of Islamic facts on generic sheets (pillar order,
  F1/Twinkl matching). No Islamic fill-in review text was readable.

## 3. Our Activity 3 checked against the market

Our page (Letter, viewed 2026-10-17): heading "Fill in the missing word", instruction "Circle it,
then write it on the line." One sentence (Nunito 700, 20px) with an inline blank, then three choices
in a row (Baloo 2, 24px bold): house / religion / garden. Source for the sentence is in the footer.

- **Good:** one sentence, short; 3 choices (right for ages 6–8, F6); choices big and bold; the
  sentence names who said it (Shaykh Ibn Baz) and the footer gives the real book and printed page —
  no competitor seen does this.
- **Weak points:**
  1. **The blank is too short to write in.** It is ~1.2 in wide at text height (~0.2 in). A 6–7-year-old
     writing "religion" needs roughly 2.5–3 in of length and ~0.6–0.75 in of height (F7, F8).
     Children will write over the full stop or above the line.
  2. **Two steps in one instruction** ("Circle it, then write it") is fine for 8–10 but long for a
     6-year-old who can't read it alone; the parent must read it (acceptable, but say so in the guide).
  3. **Distractors are not plausible** ("house", "garden"): every child gets it by sense alone, so it
     tests reading, not the lesson. Fine for the youngest; weak for 8–10.
  4. The choices look like plain text, not a choice row; a child may not see they are "options".
  5. Only one item: market sheets typically have 3–6 items with one word bank.

## 4. Recommendations for our page, ranked

**Change**
1. **Give the answer its own writing line**: either widen the blank to ~2.75–3 in, or (better) put a
   separate write line under the sentence, ~3 in long with ~0.7 in of vertical space (Letter and A4).
   Highest impact; easy.
2. **Make the options look like options**: put each choice in a light outlined rounded box (thin
   dark-grey line, no fill, to save ink), evenly spaced, with "Circle one:" before the row. Keep 3 options.
3. **Distractors**: for the 8–10 band use plausible options drawn only from verified lesson
   vocabulary (owner / source-researcher chooses; design gives the slot). Keep 3 options on every
   circle-the-answer item so guessing odds stay the same (F6).

**Keep**
4. Three choices, short sentence, the scholar named in the sentence, the real book and printed page
   in the footer "Source for parents" line.
5. Verb-first heading ("Fill in the missing word").

**Add**
6. When a lesson has more than one verified sentence, use 2–3 items sharing one 3–4-word bank
   (market norm), rather than one item per activity. Never pad with unsourced sentences — drop the
   item if there is no verified source.
7. Answer key: show the circled word and the written word on a reduced copy of the page, plus the
   source line (CLAUDE.md). Mention "includes answer key with sources" in the listing — keys are
   rare among Islamic Etsy sheets (F4).
8. Teaching-guide note: "Read the instruction aloud for children under 7."

## 5. Warnings: competitor patterns that break our rules

- **Unsourced statements**: Islamic fill-in sheets in snippets give sentences with no source; one
  matching sheet drew a reviewer correction on pillar order (F1). We must never use a sentence
  without a verified source; if the source fails, the item is dropped.
- **Character art on "All About Me / My Faith" sheets**: these often show children; could not
  verify images, but treat as a pattern to avoid (people drawings).
- **"Faceless" figures** are still drawings of people; not allowed for us.
- No music or bid'ah items seen in fill-in snippets (but holiday-themed packs, e.g. non-Islamic
  holidays, appear in the same Etsy categories — avoid any such theme).
