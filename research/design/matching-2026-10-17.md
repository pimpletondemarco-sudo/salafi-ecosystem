## 1. Common matching patterns in the market

| Pattern | What the evidence shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Picture ↔ word** (most common) | Arabic vocabulary sets mostly match a picture to a word ("find the picture for a word and draw a line"); Etsy picture lists include a camel and a dog. Twinkl's Five Pillars sheet matches each pillar to a picture. | M3, M4, M6 |
| **Word ↔ meaning (Arabic ↔ English)** | Rarer. Kalimah's free sheet has students match Arabic words to English translations after first reviewing a vocabulary list. This is our format. | M3 |
| **Word ↔ letters / spelling** | learnarabicworksheets.com: look at a word on the left, draw a line to the right group of letters (10-page PDF). | M3 |
| **Cut-and-paste matching** | Twinkl offers a cut-and-paste version of its Five Pillars matching sheet as an alternative to drawing lines. | M6 |
| **Matching inside a mixed workbook** | Goodword's Hadith Activity Book ends each chapter with "fill in the blanks… match the following". Big Etsy packs (200+ pages) list matching beside tracing, colouring and puzzles. | M5, M7 |
| **Small sets / chunking** | An educator blog suggests splitting two-column draw-a-line sheets into groups of about four, so young children don't face ten matches at once. Another guide recommends at most ~8 items per skill. | M8, M9 |
| **Price context** | Single Etsy matching sheets seen at $2–$3 (e.g. "Pillars of Faith – Iman" worksheet $2.00; Arabic alphabet matching $3.00; Arabic letters colouring & matching $5.00). | M7, M10 |

## 3. Our Activity 2 checked against the market

Our page (Letter, viewed 2026-10-17): three Arabic words on the left (Noto Naskh ~38px), a green dot
after each, a ~4-inch gap, then a green dot and the shuffled English meaning (Nunito 800, 20px).
Rows are ~0.67 inch apart. Instruction: "Draw a line from each word to its meaning."

- **Good / market-normal:** word↔meaning format (rarer than picture matching, and the only kind that
  fits our no-living-beings rule without effort); big start/end dots; wide gap so lines are easy;
  shuffled so no pair is straight across; short verb-first instruction; plenty of white space.
- **Weak points:**
  1. **Only 3 pairs and no extra option**: once two lines are drawn, the third is automatic, so
     only two real choices are made.
  2. **The answers are printed just above**: the tracing rows label each word "My Lord / My
     Religion / My Prophet", so a child can copy the match instead of remembering it. That is
     fine as scaffolding for a 6-year-old, but it does not test an 8–10-year-old.
  3. **Arabic words in the match are smaller (~38px) than in tracing (62px)** and the harakat sit
     close together; still readable, but the smallest Arabic on the page.
  4. Nothing tells the parent where the answers are (the answer key exists, but the page does not
     point to it).

## 4. Recommendations for our page, ranked

**Keep**
1. Word ↔ meaning matching with dots at both ends and a wide (≥3.5 in) gap. It suits ages 6–10,
   avoids pictures of living beings, and is less common than picture matching.
2. Shuffled order with no straight-across pair; short instruction "Draw a line from each word to its meaning."
3. Arabic on the left, English on the right (children read the English prompt side last, the line
   goes left→right, which suits English-reading 6–10-year-olds; the Arabic word itself stays RTL).

**Change**
4. **Add one extra English option on the right (4 options for 3 words)** so the last match is not
   automatic. The extra option must come from verified lesson vocabulary chosen by the owner /
   source-researcher (a design slot only; I do not choose religious wording).
5. **Enlarge the Arabic in the match to ~46–50px** and give each row ~0.8 in height so harakat
   are clear; keep the English at 20px.
6. **Don't let the same page give the answers away**: either move the English meanings in the
   tracing rows to small grey text under the model (fits the tracing report's rec #1), or, for the
   older band (8–10), put the matching on a review page later in the workbook where no labels are
   visible. Owner choice: scaffolded (current) vs testing.

**Add**
7. A tiny footer note or answer-key page reference ("Answers: Answer Key p. X") so parents find it.
8. Answer key page: show the page reduced with the correct lines drawn in, plus the source line
   for each religious answer (CLAUDE.md requirement).
9. Later (not for this page): an optional cut-and-match strip at the back of the workbook for
   younger children who can't yet draw straight lines (market has cut-and-paste variants, M6).

## 5. Warnings: competitor patterns that break our rules (selling points for us)

- **Picture matching with animals**: Arabic vocabulary picture sets list a camel and a dog among
  the pictures (Etsy snippet, M3). Animal drawings break our rule.
- **Pictures for pillars**: Twinkl's Five Pillars matching uses pictures for each pillar (M6). I could
  not see the pictures; such sets commonly show people praying or giving — check, never copy.
- **"Faceless" people are still people**: some Islamic sellers advertise faceless characters (M7,
  and research/market-2026-10-16.md). Under our rule (Ibn Baz, al-Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan) drawings of
  living beings are avoided altogether, so "faceless" is not a fit for us. We can say in listings
  "no drawings of people or animals".
- **Unsourced content**: matching pages typically state facts with no source; one drew a public
  question on order of the pillars (M6). Our footer source line and cited answer key answer this.
- No music or bid'ah content was seen in matching snippets.
