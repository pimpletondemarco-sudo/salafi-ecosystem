## 1. Common patterns in the market

| Pattern | What the evidence shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully scripted lessons for untrained parents** | The selling point of the best-known homeschool programmes: *Ordinary Parent's Guide to Teaching Reading* is "scripted… requires no special training"; *All About Reading* teacher's manuals guide "through the scripted steps of each lesson"; The Good and the Beautiful: "fully scripted. You read aloud…". | G1, G2, G3 |
| **Teacher-only overview box at the top** | All About Reading: a coloured (cream) box gives an overview of the lesson "meant only for you, the teacher", then fixed sections: review, new teaching, read-aloud, progress record. | G2 |
| **Fixed lesson sections in the same order** | Ascension's leader guide: objectives, opening, outline of the presentation, discussion questions, activity ideas. Truth78 GIFT parent pages: one page per lesson with a summary, discussion questions and an activity. Seton religion plans: day-by-day read-aloud and discussion steps with the week/day marked "in a circle at the top". | G4, G5, G6 |
| **Short parent companion vs. thick manual** | Truth78 GIFT: one page per lesson ($1.99 for 40 lessons). Islamic classroom guides are long and sold separately: Safar teacher's guides 66–140 pages, described as "a working document… suggestions… can be adapted"; ISF *Learning Islam* guide gives "key content summaries of each lesson" and answers (~$32); ISF *I Love Islam* parent/teacher guides ~$27–31. | G5, G7, G8 |
| **Islamic student books: small lesson chunks** | *Weekend Learning* Level 1 (ages 5–7): each lesson opens with a colouring page to settle the child, short sections teach 2–3 concepts, then an in-class review. | G9 |
| **Free Islamic workbooks rely on a teacher** | A2 (Manchester Salafi School) is "used in conjunction with" separate lesson plans and tells the child "the teacher will take you through this part". No parent guide is included in A1–A3. | A1–A3 |
| **Arabic + meaning presentation** | Darussalam *60 Golden Hadith*: "Arabic text bold, Urdu/English side-by-side below", with notes "ideal for parents"; *20 Hadith for Kids*: Arabic, English meaning, brief commentary. | G10 |
| **Pronunciation help** | Children's publishers use doubled vowels (aa, ee, oo) for long sounds and admit a trade-off between easy reading and exact sounds; inconsistent spellings across books confuse parents; some add audio. | G11 |
| **Etsy Islamic printables** | Only small "parent's guide" add-ons seen (e.g. a Gumroad worksheet on Allah the Creator with "a parent's guide"); no Etsy aqeedah/tawheed listing with a full teaching guide found (also market-fit check, 2026-10-16). | G12 |

## 2. What parents praise and complain about

**Praise**
- "No special training" / confidence for parents new to the subject (G1).
- The manual "clearly lays out what to do… and how to explain each concept" (G2, homeschool review).
- New homeschoolers value hand-holding: one forum poster noted Heart of Dakota has "way more hand
  holding" than programmes that only give prompts, and some parents want exactly that (G13).

**Complaints**
- **Too wordy / read word for word:** a Shurley English reviewer called it "too wordy; too
  restrictive", disliking reading it "like a storybook, word for word" (G14). Another on the same page
  found it "repetitious, but thorough and easy to follow" — opinions split.
- **Prep time:** even fully scripted lessons "still need to read the material thoroughly beforehand";
  lesson 20–45 min, prep can be longer (G14).
- **Flipping:** parent overview "in the back of every unit book" means "constant flipping back and
  forth" (G15).
- **Repetitive daily lessons** (G15).
- **Islamic-specific:** buyers worry about accuracy of Islamic facts on generic sheets (fill-in report
  F1). Transliteration inconsistency confuses parents (G11).

## 3. Our Lesson 3 draft checked against the market

Sections today: Goal → Background (2 min) → From the Scholars (4 quotes: Arabic, "Meaning", source) →
Say it like this (4-line script) → Say the Arabic words (table) → If your child asks (3 Q&A with
sources) → Review game.

- **Ahead of the market:** fully scripted for a non-expert parent, pronunciation table, sourced Q&A,
  a game — no Islamic competitor seen offers this. Real book + printed page on every quote.
- **Gaps against the best-selling pattern:** no at-a-glance overview box (time, materials, which
  student pages); the long "From the Scholars" block sits *before* the script, so a parent must read
  scholarly Arabic before they reach "what do I say"; no page cross-references to the student pages
  and answer key; no "you may say this in your own words" note (wordiness complaint).

## 4. Recommendations for OUR teaching guide pages, ranked

**Change / add (most important first)**
1. **Two pages per lesson, always the same order, each lesson starting on a new page.**
   Page 1 = teach it: overview box → Background → Say it like this → Say the Arabic words → Review game.
   Page 2 = go deeper: If your child asks → From the Scholars.
   A parent can teach from page 1 alone; page 2 is for questions and checking. (Market: one-page parent
   companions and fixed-order sections, G4–G6; CLAUDE.md sections all kept.)
2. **"Before you start" overview box at the top** (light bone fill or outline, rounded): Goal (one
   sentence), Time (about 15–20 min), You need (printed pages, pencil, crayons, game items), Student
   pages p. X–Y, Answers p. Z. (AAR teacher box, G2; fixes flipping, G15.)
3. **Script formatting:** a green "Say:" label and the parent's words in bold; actions in plain text
   ("Point to the three roots."). Short lines (aim ≤ 20 words), 4–6 lines per lesson. One line in
   "How to use this guide": "Read the bold lines aloud, or say them in your own words." (Answers the
   "too wordy / word for word" complaint, G14.)
4. **Reading level:** plain English for any adult (about grade 6–8 reading level); every Arabic or
   Islamic term explained the first time it appears; no unexplained abbreviations.
5. **Pronunciation table kept, made consistent:** Arabic in Noto Sans Arabic, fully voweled and large
   (about 22–26 pt); simple respelling with the stressed part in capitals (as in the draft); one
   pronunciation key on a front page (aa / ee / oo, "th" as in *think*, etc.) used identically in every
   lesson (G11).
6. **"From the Scholars" as a boxed panel** (thin green outline, white inside to save ink), one quote
   per block: Arabic right-aligned in Noto Sans Arabic → "Meaning of Shaykh X's words: …" → small
   citation line *title*, scholar, publisher, edition, printed page (**no year**). Ayat in their own
   block, text only from quran.ai (King Fahd Complex), Hilali-Khan meaning, labelled. Keep internal
   notes (e.g. "digital copy has a typing slip") out of the customer file or in the internal source
   file only.
7. **"If your child asks…":** question in bold, answer in 1–3 plain sentences, short source in
   brackets. Keep 2–4 questions per lesson.
8. **Review game:** no preparation beyond paper and pencil, 5 minutes, steps numbered 1–3, with a
   tiny line drawing of objects only (paper leaves, roots, cards). (G9: lessons end with a review.)
9. **Front matter (2–3 pages):** "How to use this guide" (the lesson routine in 4 steps), the
   pronunciation key, and a contents table listing each lesson's guide, student and answer pages.
10. **Separate PDF file** for the guide as well as inside the full book, so parents can read it on a
    phone or tablet while the child works on paper (same reasoning as the answer key).
11. **Low ink:** white pages; colour only in the rounded header/footer panels (0.3 in inside the
    edge) and thin box outlines; no full-colour backgrounds behind text.

**Keep**
12. All the CLAUDE.md sections (goal, 2-minute background, script, pronunciation, Q&A with sources,
    review game, From the Scholars) and the honest "we will learn this in the next lessons" approach
    when the lesson's sources do not cover a question.

**Listing**
13. Advertise "Teaching guide for parents — no Arabic needed — every point sourced": no Etsy Islamic
    listing seen offers a scripted, sourced guide (G12; market-fit check 2026-10-16).

## 5. Warnings: competitor patterns that break our rules

- **Unsourced content:** A2 prints hadith as "(Narrated in Muslim)" without number or grading and an
  unsourced saying in every footer; Safar describes its guide as "suggestions… adapted by the teacher".
  Our guide may only contain points from the lesson's verified sources; anything else is dropped.
- **Songs, chants with rhythm or "music time" in lesson routines** are common in general kids'
  curricula (general observation; not verified in a specific guide here). Our review games use no
  music or instruments.
- **Story characters and illustrated children** in Islamic lessons (A1 contents: "Zaid Learns How To
  Pray"; Darussalam books describe "kid-friendly illustrations" — not checked whether they show
  people). We use no people, faces or animals, including in game instructions and icons.
- **Celebration-themed activities** (birthdays, non-Islamic holidays, mawlid) appear in general and
  some Islamic activity packs; never in our games or examples.
- **Reading a scholar's words loosely:** competitors paraphrase without saying whose words they are.
  We always label "Meaning of Shaykh X's words" and give the printed page.
